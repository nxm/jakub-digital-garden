# Good habits

> Framework: every good habit is built with the 4 laws — **make it obvious, make it attractive, make it easy, make it satisfying**. Every bad habit is broken with the inversions — **make it invisible, make it unattractive, make it difficult, make it unsatisfying**.

## 1. Health

**What does it mean for me?**
- My body is fit
- My HRV is good (75–100 ms)
- My mind is calm

**How can I accomplish that?**
- Regular workouts and running
- Good sleep (7–8 h)
- Less stress
- Routine blood tests

**Make it obvious (cue)**
- Lay out my running outfit the night before, next to the bed
- Keep a full water bottle on my desk
- Put workouts in the calendar at fixed times, like real appointments
- Keep the fruit bowl on the counter and the snacks out of sight

**Make it attractive (craving)**
- Good food = a sharp, sexy body
- Sport = a fit and healthy body I'm proud of
- Sleep = full energy and a clear mind
- Pair training with something I like (a good playlist, training with friends)

**Make it easy (response)**
- Keep only healthy food in the fridge — no snacks, so junk food is never an option
- Keep no alcohol or cigarettes in the apartment
- Book workouts as appointments with friends so I can't back out
- Prep the running outfit in advance — zero friction to start
- Fixed sleep rule: to wake at 8:30, I'm in bed by 1:10 at the latest (7 h + 20 min buffer). No excuses.

**Make it satisfying (reward)**
- Track every workout and every night of 7 h+ sleep on a habit calendar — never break the chain
- Check my HRV weekly and watch it climb into the 75–100 ms range
- After a training week, reward myself with something healthy I enjoy (a good meal out, a massage)

## 2. Self-improvement

**What does it mean for me?**
- I actively develop my identity
- I am a little better each day
- I learn how to be a better person

**How can I accomplish that?**
- Reading books
- Listening to audiobooks
- Reading blogs
- Talking with smart people

**Make it obvious (cue)**
- Keep the current book on my pillow / nightstand so it's the last thing I see
- Queue an audiobook for every commute and every walk
- Habit stack: "After my morning coffee, I read 10 pages."

**Make it attractive (craving)**
- I am the kind of person who reads and grows — every page is a vote for that identity
- Join a book club or a group of ambitious people so learning feels social
- Talk about what I learn — teaching makes it exciting

**Make it easy (response)**
- Start absurdly small: 10 pages a day, or 1 chapter of an audiobook
- Keep a book in every bag so there's always one within reach
- Two-minute rule: the goal is just to open the book, not to finish it

**Make it satisfying (reward)**
- Keep a reading log / notes vault — visible proof of progress
- Track a streak of daily reading
- Write one takeaway per book and apply it — see the ideas pay off in real life

## 3. Daily life organization

**What does it mean for me?**
- My home is always tidy
- I always have food to eat
- My clothes are always clean
- I stay on top of routine tasks like the car inspection

**How can I accomplish that?**
- Consistency — I eat dinner, I clean up right after
- Planning — I know what I'll eat the next day
- A small daily clean, ~10 minutes with the vacuum
- A calendar for recurring tasks (car inspection, service, etc.)

**Make it obvious (cue)**
- Keep the vacuum where I can see it, not buried in a closet
- Recurring calendar reminders for car inspection, bills, and cleaning
- Habit stack: "After dinner, I clean the kitchen."

**Make it attractive (craving)**
- I am an organized, put-together person — a tidy space reflects a tidy mind
- A clean home feels calm and lets me relax
- Play music or a podcast while I tidy so it's enjoyable

**Make it easy (response)**
- The two-minute rule: never leave a room without putting one thing back
- Cook once, plan tomorrow's meals in the same session
- Keep the daily clean to 10 minutes — small enough to never skip
- Lay out clothes for the week on Sunday

**Make it satisfying (reward)**
- Tick each recurring task off the calendar
- Enjoy the reset feeling of a clean space every evening
- Track the "10-minute clean" streak

## 4. Business

**What does it mean for me?**
- I earn a living
- I'm organized across my projects (I know what's going on)
- I'm financially literate (I know what to do with money)
- I ship what I need to ship
- I'm a good leader
- I'm a good speaker / presenter

**How can I accomplish that?**
- Consistency
- Good planning
- A responsible team
- Following solid sources on what to do with money
- Learning from people who run businesses
- Selling to people — not being afraid to take money
- Reaching out to people
- Staying organized — if I said I'd do it, I do it
- Being respected
- Practicing fluency in my native and second language
- Being public, so people trust me

**Make it obvious (cue)**
- Start each day by writing the top 3 things I must ship
- Keep a single dashboard / board with the status of every project
- Block fixed time for deep work, outreach, and learning
- Habit stack: "After my morning review, I send one outreach message."

**Make it attractive (craving)**
- I am a founder and a leader — every shipped task is a vote for that identity
- Surround myself with other builders so ambition feels normal
- Frame selling as helping: I'm giving people something valuable, and the money is fair exchange

**Make it easy (response)**
- Break every project into the next single concrete action
- Templates for outreach messages and proposals — no starting from scratch
- One outreach message a day, minimum — small enough to always do
- Rehearse presentations out loud, one short reps at a time

**Make it satisfying (reward)**
- Track shipped tasks and closed deals somewhere visible
- Review revenue weekly — watch the number grow
- Celebrate wins publicly (share progress), which also builds trust
- Keep promises visibly — a reputation for reliability compounds

# Bad habits

## 1. Cigarettes

**Why do I do it?**
- A strange urge
- Social pull — if someone smokes, I want to smoke too
- It "goes better" with alcohol
- It relieves stress for a second

**Advantages:** none

**Disadvantages:**
- Worse physical fitness
- Yellow teeth
- It stinks
- It makes me unhealthy (cancer risk)

**Make it invisible (cue)**
- Keep no cigarettes in the apartment, car, or bag
- Avoid the smoking corner at parties; stand with the non-smokers
- Cut alcohol, which is the main trigger

**Make it unattractive (craving)**
- I am a healthy, athletic person — smokers are not who I want to be
- Reframe it: yellow teeth, stinking clothes, ruined lungs
- Remember it gives me nothing — zero real advantages

**Make it difficult (response)**
- Never carry cigarettes or a lighter — force a walk to a shop to get one
- Tell friends I've quit, so asking for one is embarrassing
- Put a real cost on it (a bet / forfeit with a friend for every cigarette)

**Make it unsatisfying (reward)**
- Track "days smoke-free" and refuse to break the streak
- Put the money I'd spend on cigarettes into a visible jar / savings goal
- Ask a friend to hold me accountable — reporting a slip stings

## 2. Alcohol

**Why do I do it?**
- Sometimes the same strange urge
- Social pull
- It's seen as relaxing

**Advantages:** none

**Disadvantages:**
- It kills brain cells
- I lose my mental clarity
- The hangover is brutal
- It's risky — bad things can happen after drinking

**Make it invisible (cue)**
- Keep no alcohol at home
- Order a non-alcoholic drink first at social events so I'm never empty-handed
- Avoid the "let's grab a drink" default — suggest coffee, food, or activity instead

**Make it unattractive (craving)**
- I am someone with a clear, sharp mind — drinking is the opposite of that
- Picture the hangover and the wasted next day before the first drink
- Remember it offers no real advantage

**Make it difficult (response)**
- Be the designated driver — a built-in reason not to drink
- Set a hard drink limit before going out and tell a friend
- Don't keep cash / card easy for buying rounds

**Make it unsatisfying (reward)**
- Track alcohol-free days
- Notice and log how good the clear-headed mornings feel
- Accountability partner for nights out

## 3. Doom-scrolling

**Why do I do it?**
- FOMO
- Dopamine
- Sometimes I genuinely need to look something up

**Advantages:** occasionally something is inspiring

**Disadvantages:** a waste of time

**Make it invisible (cue)**
- Delete the worst apps from my phone, or log out after each use
- Move social apps off the home screen into a hidden folder
- Grayscale mode — a dull screen is far less tempting
- Keep the phone out of the bedroom and out of reach while working

**Make it unattractive (craving)**
- I am someone who spends attention on purpose, not by reflex
- Reframe it: this is someone else's business monetizing my time
- Remember the feeling after an hour lost — empty, not inspired

**Make it difficult (response)**
- App timers / screen-time limits with friction to override
- Log out after every session so re-entry takes effort
- Leave the phone in another room during work and before bed

**Make it unsatisfying (reward)**
- Track screen time weekly and aim to lower it
- Replace the scroll with a better default (a book within reach)
- Tell myself the trade honestly: those minutes came from sleep, training, or work

## 4. Junk food

**Why do I do it?**
- It's easy — ready to eat
- Weak cooking skills
- It's tempting sometimes

**Advantages:** occasionally fine — it can save time

**Disadvantages:**
- Cholesterol
- Too many calories
- Feeling tired and heavy afterward, especially after fatty food (pizza, burgers)

**Make it invisible (cue)**
- Don't buy it — if it's not in the apartment, I can't eat it
- Keep healthy food prepped and visible; keep junk out of sight
- Don't shop hungry, and shop from a list

**Make it unattractive (craving)**
- I am an athlete who fuels his body — junk food works against that
- Remember the heavy, tired feeling after a fatty meal
- Picture the cholesterol and the sluggish afternoon

**Make it difficult (response)**
- Meal-prep so the healthy option is the easy one
- Learn 3–4 simple, fast recipes so cooking isn't a skill barrier
- No junk food in the house means a deliberate trip is needed to get it

**Make it satisfying — inverted, make it unsatisfying (reward)**
- Track clean-eating days
- Notice how much better I feel and train when I eat well
- Log meals so the slips are visible

## 5. Irregular sleep patterns

**Why do I do it?**
- I tell myself I'm a night person — probably just an excuse
- "I need to do this and it can't wait until tomorrow"

**Advantages:** none

**Disadvantages:** many

**Make it invisible (cue)**
- Set a fixed "wind-down" alarm every night, not just a wake alarm
- Dim the lights and put screens away an hour before bed
- Keep the phone charging outside the bedroom

**Make it unattractive (craving)**
- I am someone who protects his energy and mind — sleep is how I do it
- Reframe late nights as stealing from tomorrow's focus and training
- Remember: nothing "urgent" at midnight is actually urgent

**Make it difficult (response)**
- Hard rule: in bed 7 h + 20 min before wake time, no exceptions
- Screens out of the bedroom so there's nothing to stay up for
- Front-load "can't-wait" tasks earlier in the day so they don't spill into the night

**Make it satisfying (reward)**
- Track hours slept and the wake-up time on a habit calendar
- Notice the energy and clarity of a well-rested morning and log it
- Never-break-the-chain streak for consistent bedtimes

> [!NOTE] Atomic Habits — most important things

## 3 key lessons from Atomic Habits

### Lesson 1: Small habits make a big difference

Improving by 1 percent isn't particularly notable — sometimes it isn't even noticeable — but it can be far more meaningful, especially in the long run. If you get 1 percent better each day for a year, you'll end up 37 times better by the time you're done.

### Lesson 2: Forget about setting goals — focus on your system instead

Goals are about the results you want to achieve. Systems are about the processes that lead to those results.

### Lesson 3: Build identity-based habits

The key to building lasting habits is focusing on creating a new identity first. Your current behaviors are simply a reflection of your current identity. What you do now is a mirror image of the type of person you believe you are.

To change your behavior for good, you need to start believing new things about yourself:
1. Decide the type of person you want to be.
2. Prove it to yourself with small wins.

Your identity emerges out of your habits. Every action is a vote for the type of person you wish to become.

The cue triggers a craving, which motivates a response, which provides a reward, which satisfies the craving and ultimately becomes associated with the cue. Together, these four steps form a neurological feedback loop — cue, craving, response, reward — that ultimately lets you create automatic habits. This cycle is known as [the habit loop](https://jamesclear.com/three-steps-habit-change).

**How to create a good habit:**
1. The 1st law (cue): Make it obvious
2. The 2nd law (craving): Make it attractive
3. The 3rd law (response): Make it easy
4. The 4th law (reward): Make it satisfying

**How to break a bad habit:**
1. Inversion of the 1st law (cue): Make it invisible
2. Inversion of the 2nd law (craving): Make it unattractive
3. Inversion of the 3rd law (response): Make it difficult
4. Inversion of the 4th law (reward): Make it unsatisfying
